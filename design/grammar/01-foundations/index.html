Grammar 1: Foundations

The parts every Aber object will be built from: ground, ink, type, radius, elevation, glyph tiles, chips, tags, numbers and buttons. Jig 2 assembles them into the six objects; this page only proves the parts.

  • The dial panel (top right) tunes ground warmth, radius, tint strength, body size and shadow softness live across the whole page. Its Copy values action copies the token names.
  • Three choices are open, marked Choose. Everything else is a proposal to react to with the note fields or Agentation.

2. Elevation

Shadow means "above" and nothing else. The more temporary a surface, the higher it sits. Toast and tooltip turn to ink so they read above everything without a heavier shadow.

3. Object glyphs

One glyph per object, on a pale tint of its hue. The tint is the only per-type colour. Regular weight at rest, fill when selected, with the 1.5px ink ring. Suggestion takes Sparkle, no hue and a dashed edge: it is a proposed change, not a thing of its own, and dashed already means "not yet mine". Click a tile to select it.

RestHoverSelected
A Tinted tile

Type reads at a glance in mixed lists and gives selection a shape to ring. Costs 28px of width and a little colour on every row.

B Bare glyph in the hue

Quieter; the list is mostly text. Types are told apart by glyph shape and its colour alone, so mixed lists scan slower.

5. Buttons

One solid ink primary per surface. Secondary is a quiet fill. Icon buttons name themselves and their shortcut in a tooltip (hover or focus; one is pinned open here). Dismiss sits left of Accept; X only ever closes something fleeting.

A Pills

Reads as a button anywhere, distinct from rows and chips, which are rounded rectangles.

B Rounded rectangles

Shares the radius family with rows and chips. Tighter, more tool-like; buttons and chips look more alike.
