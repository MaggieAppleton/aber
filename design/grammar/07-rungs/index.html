Grammar 07: Moving between levels

Proposed. How you move from a preview to its peek, from the peek to the full view, and back, in the type-first default view and in Columns' docked pane. Built only from 00–06 and Navigation: the components are 06's, the tiers and origins are 04's, the timings are 05's 90 and 160ms and 11's 380ms pane spring.

Decide (Maggie): three open choices

  1. How Open moves you to the full view Recommend a. The tile and title you were looking at become the full view's title, so you never lose the thing you chose. b is calmer but says only "you went deeper".
  2. Where a peek sits Recommend a. Beside the row leaves the rows under it visible, so you can keep pointing down the list while the peek follows. It falls back to below when there's no room.
  3. Where Back lands Recommend a. A peek lasts a moment (04: persistence "moment"), so Back returns to the preview with focus on it. b restores the peek exactly as you left it.

Already decided, applied here

  • A peek opens on a 500ms hover or a click, grows from its preview and shows the essentials and relations; Open moves the rail and list to the object; Back returns (Navigation 3, 06).
  • Peeks are tier 4: tethered, crisp shadow (--sh-pop), radius 10, z 40. One transient layer at a time; a link inside a peek replaces it (04 rule 7).
  • Tethered surfaces take 160ms on the spring; hover and press 90ms; the docked pane 380ms on the spring with contents fading in over 160ms (05 rule 4, 11 rule 3).
  • Reduced motion swaps movement for a cross-fade. X only closes transient surfaces.

The rules

  1. Every move starts from the thing you acted on and returns to it. A peek grows from its trigger's tile. Open carries that tile and title to the full view's title. Back carries them home again.
  2. One peek, ever. Pointing at another preview while a peek is open slides the same card to it after 150ms. A preview inside the peek replaces it in place. Nothing stacks.
  3. Hover is forgiving. Moving from the trigger toward its peek never closes it or swaps it, even across other rows (the grace area). Leaving both closes it after 300ms.
  4. Click commits. A clicked peek stays until Esc, a click elsewhere or a second click on its trigger. Hover never takes focus; Esc always returns focus to the trigger.
  5. Duration follows distance. 90ms for what leaves, 160ms for a peek, 380ms for anything that crosses the window (the title travelling, the pane docking). Selecting in a list doesn't animate at all: it's the most frequent move.
  6. Interrupt anything. Every animation starts from where things are on screen now; a new move cancels the running one. Rapid pointing or clicking never queues.
  7. Reduced motion keeps the map. Nothing scales or travels; things fade where they land. Anchors, destinations and focus are unchanged.

Try it

The default view opens on Briefing. Columns is one click away in the view switcher at the rail foot, or below. Both follow the three picks.

Show origins marks each surface's origin, where moves start and land, and the hover grace area.
  • Peek: rest on a row in the main area for half a second, or click it. Then point down the rows.
  • Grace: from a row, move diagonally into its peek across the rows below. It stays put.
  • Chain: click a row inside a peek. The same card shows the next object.
  • Open: the Open button, ⌘↵, or Enter on a focused row.
  • Back: the Back button, Esc or ⌘[. The list scroll and selection come back.
  • Keys: Tab to a row, ↑ ↓ to move, Space or → to peek (it follows the arrows), Tab into it, Esc to close.

Columns: the docked pane

Click a theory card: the pane pushes in from the right edge and Prototypes folds to a rail. Another card changes the pane's contents in place. Esc or the X closes it the way it came. Rows in the pane peek and open like the default view's.

a The peek's tile and title travel to the full view's title

380ms on the spring. The peek's surface fades in 90ms; the rest of the full view fades in after it. Back runs the same path in reverse.

b The old view fades out; the full view slides in 16px from the right

90ms out, 260ms in on the spring. Direction says deeper (from the right) or back (from the left); nothing in particular is followed.

Both grow from the trigger's tile and slide along when you point at the next row. Each window opens on Cost of intelligence's full view.

a Beside the row, its tile level with the row's tile

The rows below stay visible, so pointing down the list walks the peek along it. Falls back to below where there's no room (Columns' pane, narrow windows). Chips always peek below: their siblings sit beside them.

b Below the row, its tile on the row's tile column

Reads like a menu and fits any width, but covers the rows under the trigger; flips above near the bottom.

a On the preview you opened from, with focus on it

The title travels back into the row. Scroll and selection are restored; the peek, a moment's surface, is not.

b In the peek you opened from, open again

The title travels back into the peek's head. Exactly where you were; Esc once more closes it.

Screen readers

  • The trigger is a button with aria-expanded and aria-controls; the peek is a non-modal role="dialog" named by its object.
  • Open moves focus to the full view's title (a heading, tabindex="-1") and a polite status says what opened. Back returns focus to the preview.
  • A keyboard peek is announced with how to enter, open and close it. Hover peeks are not announced and never take focus.

Narrow screens (below 640)

  • A peek becomes a bottom sheet rising from the bottom edge (04 rule 10); its origin is the edge, and it leaves the same way.
  • Open pushes the full view in from the right over the list; Back slides it out to the right. No hover; a tap peeks.
