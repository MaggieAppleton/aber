The rules (decided 4 Nov: Regular, separate cards)

  1. One base unit: 4px. Every padding, gap and height is a step on the scale 2 4 6 8 10 12 16 20 24 32 40. Nothing else. 2 is for optical nudges only.
  2. Gaps climb a ladder, and each level is at least 1.5× the one inside it (2× at Regular), so grouping reads without boxes: --gap-tight 4 (one unit: label over value, icon and count), --gap-item 8 (siblings in a group), --gap-group 16 (groups in a region), --gap-section 32 (sections).
  3. Icon to text is its own gap: --gap-icon 6, a touch more than letters need.
  4. Insets grow with the container: --inset-chip 8, --inset-control 10, --inset-row 8, --inset-card 12. Inside a card or control, the inset is never smaller than the largest gap inside it.
  5. Padding grows with focus: navigation < list < pane. The rail is most compact (--inset-nav 12 top, 8 sides), the main column a step up (--inset-list 12, 16), the pane where you read and work most generous (--inset-pane 20, 24). The rail and main column share a top line; the pane is its own surface and starts lower. Canvases (Atlas, Timeline) sit at list level, --inset-canvas 16. Floating controls (zoom, filter, view switcher) sit --inset-float 12 from the window edge in every view.
  6. Two heights for things you click: --row 32 for list and rail rows, --control 28 for buttons and inputs (--control-sm 24 inside dense rows). Every one has a 40px hit area, extended invisibly when the mark is smaller.
  7. Text inputs pad to centre their text in --control, with horizontal padding = vertical + 4.
  8. Separate with space first. Use a gap between things that are separate objects. Use a hairline divider only between uniform rows inside one container. Use a container only when the group is an object you act on, or needs its own surface level.
