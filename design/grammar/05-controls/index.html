The rules (proposed)

  1. One anatomy per control (the table below). A control is built from the height, inset, radius, label role and icon slot named there and nothing else.
  2. Two heights. 28 everywhere (--control); 24 only inside a dense row: an evidence row, a field row, a list group label (--control-sm). Chips are 24. Menu items are rows, 32. The composer is a floating bar, 40. Every target has a 40 hit area.
  3. Every control has the same seven states, with the same treatment everywhere (the state matrix). Hover is a 5% layer and ink; press keeps it and shrinks to 0.98; focus is the 2px jade ring 2 out (inside, at −2, in menus, segments and list rows); disabled is 40%; loading keeps the width and swaps the label for a spinner.
  4. Motion: hover and press 90ms; selection, segments and tethered surfaces 160ms; all on cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1), menus and peeks on the spring. The focus ring never animates. Never transition: all. Reduced motion drops the scale and keeps the fades.
  5. One primary per surface (pane, peek, modal, composer, row). Secondary for an alternative on the same surface (Open, Cancel, Dismiss). Quiet for navigation (Back) and toolbars. Icon-only for frequent verbs in the icon vocabulary.
  6. Dismiss sits left of Accept; Discard left of Accept for drafts. This pair is the one place two text buttons sit together.
  7. Reversible means do it, then offer Undo. Remove, retire (a menu of reasons), discard, merge and move to the bin happen at once with an Undo toast. Only what can't be undone asks first: deleting a dump is the one confirmation, a modal whose button names the verb and object ("Delete dump"), never "OK".
  8. Labels: verb first, sentence case, one or two words, no trailing ellipsis, concept names exact (Theory, Signal). An icon-only button's name is its vocabulary name; its tooltip adds the key in mono. A removal says what it removes in its accessible name ("Remove Agent memory from this theory").
  9. Don't disable when you can explain. Disabled controls use aria-disabled, stay focusable and say why in their tooltip. Send is the exception: it waits for text.
  10. No toggles, checkboxes or tabs bars. On/off is an icon button with aria-pressed (Fill when on); a choice of two to four is a segmented control.
