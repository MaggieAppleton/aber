Grammar nav: type-first navigation

Your brief: the sidebar lists the types (themes, theories, prototypes, signals, dumps); choosing one opens a second sidebar of everything of that type. Built with the decided colour (D), spacing (Regular) and type. Every prototype on this page is clickable.

Decide on this page

  1. Structure: item fills the main area (A), contents as boxes with the pane (B), or an icon rail with the item and pane (C)
  2. Six open concept questions: answer in the note under the structure choice

The navigation model

  1. The rail lists the types, refined to raw: Themes, Theories, Prototypes, Signals, Dumps. Each has its glyph colour and a count. Briefing sits above them as the morning entry: what changed since you last looked, across all types.
  2. The second sidebar lists one type's items, ordered for what its population can bear (table below). Small populations show everything; signals and dumps are time streams with search, and signals add a theme filter.
  3. Selecting an item shows it in the main area. How much it shows, and whether the docked pane stays, is the structure choice below.
  4. Relations show inside the item: a theme's theories and signals, a theory's evidence, rival, prototypes and what follows from it, a signal's theories and dumps, a dump's products. Following a relation either moves you (A: the rail and list follow, with a back link) or opens it in the pane so you keep your place (B, C).
  5. Columns, Atlas and Timeline are lenses, not homes. They sit at the foot of the rail in every structure, next to List (the default). A lens replaces the main area; the rail stays. Atlas and Timeline keep the second sidebar as the picker and highlight the selection. Columns hides it, because its five columns are the lists of every type, and it pulls the selection's lineage to the top.
  6. The pane, where kept, is docked right, aligned to the top, a surface with a hairline seam.

Try it

Switch types in the rail, pick items in the second sidebar, follow relations, and switch lenses at the foot of the rail.

Structure
A Item fills the main area, no pane

Three columns: type rail, item list, the item as a full page. Following a relation moves the rail and list to that object, with a back link. In a lens, a click selects the item and a small bar offers Open. Simplest and roomiest for reading and writing; you lose your place every time you follow a link, and you can't look at two things at once.

At 390

  • Type rail becomes a bottom tab bar (Briefing plus five types).
  • The item list is the screen for that tab; the item pushes in full screen from the right, with back.
  • Lenses: Atlas and Timeline from a menu in the list header; Columns is not shown on a phone.
B Contents as boxes, item opens in the pane

Four columns, closest to today: the main area shows what the selected item holds as contained cards (a theme's theories, a theory's evidence); clicking a card, or the item's own header, opens it in the docked pane. Keeps your place and the pane you like; the main area is narrow, and the selected item's own details live one click away in the pane.

At 390

  • Type rail becomes a bottom tab bar; the item list fills the screen.
  • Selecting pushes the boxes view full screen; a card opens the pane as a bottom sheet that drags up to full.
  • Lenses as in A.
C Icon rail, the item in main, relations in the pane

Rationale: icons give back the ~150px the named rail costs, enough for the item as a full page and the pane beside it. Type names move to tooltips; counts go (signals keep the new dot). You read one thing and look at a second without losing the first, at the cost of a rail you must learn.

At 390

  • The icon rail is already a tab bar: it moves to the bottom unchanged.
  • The item list fills the screen; the item pushes full screen; a relation opens as a bottom sheet over it.
  • Lenses as in A.

Open concept questions

  1. Briefing: a rail entry above the types (shown), or a fifth lens?
  2. Rail order: refined to raw (shown, themes first), or raw to refined to match Columns (dumps first)?
  3. Themes as a filter: should Theories and Signals also group or filter by theme, so themes stay a way in without being the default?
  4. Signals awaiting you: is a day stream enough, or does the list need an "Unfiled" or "Unseen" group at the top?
  5. Where lenses live: the foot of the rail (shown), or the top-right of the main area?
  6. Where Aber opens: on Briefing in the morning, or on the last type and item you had?
