Grammar 06: Objects, preview, peek, full

Proposed. Every object in Aber follows one anatomy and appears at three rungs: a preview where it's mentioned (a list row, a card, a chip), a peek that grows from that preview, and a full view that fills the main area. Built only from 00–05 and the decided type-first navigation. The list rows here replace the nav jig's placeholders.

Decide on this page

  1. Tile and text alignment Recommended c: the tile sits inside the name's first line, 2px above the capitals, so one-line and two-line rows align the same way and nothing overhangs; 18 with a 14 glyph is already 03's card-meta tile.
  2. A challenged theory on previews Recommended c: it follows "beautiful or not at all"; a challenge still can't be missed, because Briefing lists it first and the peek and full view open on the tripped tripwire.
  3. Unseen activity: nothing on objects, unread weight, or text Recommended a: Briefing home already lists what arrived since you last looked; repeating it on every object adds a mark you don't act on.
  4. Peek: essentials and relations, or essentials only Recommended a: a peek should answer "what is this tied to" without costing your place.
  5. Full view: prose first, or a fields table Recommended a: the key line under the title carries the same values with no labels; you called the label table noisy (DI-010).

Decided 4 Nov (Maggie)

  • Pending suggestions are not shown on previews. No count or mark on rows, cards or chips; suggestions appear in the peek, the full view and Briefing home. "If we can't find a beautiful way to show it, we should not show it."
  • No jade "new" dot on objects. How unseen activity shows, if at all, is choice 1.
  • Short labels are one compact line: themes and prototypes show the tile, the name in ink 2 and a bare count or status glyph at the right. Sentences (theories, signals, dumps) keep two lines.
  • No red challenge dot on previews or cards. Tripwire text is ink; only the Signpost glyph is red.
  • Counts are a bare glyph and a number, no noun: a theme's peek reads [Target] 5 [Pulse] 23 on the title's line.
  • A peek shows as many relations as fit, then fades out at the bottom (a surface-colour gradient). No scrolling, no "Show more"; Open shows everything.
  • Signals always show Pulse on their tile; kind, if shown, is text.

The object grammar

One anatomy, the same slots in the same order at every rung. The tile hangs in its own column; everything else shares the name's left edge (the two guides below).

  1. 1 Tile: the type's glyph on its tile, the same 24 tile at every level. A signal is always Pulse; its kind, if shown, is text in the key value. No type word is ever written.
  2. 2 Name: wraps (two lines in rows, three in cards, in full in a peek or full view).
  3. 3 One key value: the attribute that tells this object apart from its neighbours. A draft shows "Draft" here until accepted.
  4. 4 Status: no dots. A dump that couldn't be read says so in words on its key line. A challenge is choice 2; pending suggestions show only in the peek, full view and Briefing; unseen activity is choice 3.
  5. 5 On-demand attributes: the date appears at the right end of the key line on hover; the peek and full view add the rest to the key line.
  6. 6 Attached objects: tripwires and suggestions never stand alone. A mark on the host's preview, one line in its peek, a block with Dismiss | Accept in its full view.
  7. 7 Relations: the peek shows the most important one, up to three rows; the full view shows all of them, as the same rows (chips for themes). Rows remove with a minus on hover.
  8. If a mark can't be shown beautifully, don't show it on the preview; push it to the peek or full view.
  9. Short labels are one line; sentences are two. Themes and prototypes: tile, name in ink 2, a bare count or status glyph at the right. Theories, signals and dumps: name, then the key value under it.
  10. A row never repeats its group. Prototypes grouped by status drop the status; signals and dumps grouped by day show the day only on hover.
