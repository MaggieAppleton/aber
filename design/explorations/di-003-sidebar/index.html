DI-003 · Themes and prototypes in the sidebar

Theme sections' sidebar lists themes at the top and parks Prototypes in an accordion pinned to the bottom, with a chevron on every prototype row. Below: the current sidebar and four structures, each a live copy of the app's real CSS and captured markup at the real 232 px rail (212 px of content), with One-person app kit (p3) open in the pane.

  • Chevrons mean expand/collapse only. No proposal puts a chevron on a row that navigates.
  • Click rows, headings, tabs and section headers. Click a theme heading in the view to open that theme in the pane; the sidebar follows.
  • Use the bar to switch to the long population (14 themes, 12 prototypes with long names) and to the collapsed or overlay rail.

Current Bottom accordion

Captured markup and classes from current-sidebar.tsx. Rows navigate to the pane but end in a chevron; the open prototype has no selected state; the list sits under the view switcher's 64 px clearance.

  • The section's position says "secondary" through distance, not structure; with a long theme list it scrolls away entirely.
  • Theme rows carry no icon, prototype rows carry the type icon and a chevron: two row grammars in one rail.
  • "New prototype" is a row inside the list; "New theme" is a + in the brand row. Creation lives in two different places.
A+C Chosen direction: stacked sections with band headers

Maggie's pick: A's layout and behaviour with C's header treatment. One scroll, Themes then Prototypes, both top-aligned. Each header collapses its section and sticks, docking at the bottom when its section is below the fold. As in C, each header has the type icon on the left, a slightly bolder label and a smaller count, with + on the right. The chevron appears only on approach or while collapsed, because the header really does collapse. C's independently scrolling bands are not carried over.

  • The icons and labels on the two headers match the type icons used in Columns, so a Theme or Prototype is recognisable in both views.
  • The count is 11px against a 13px label (C's size, up from A's 12px), so it reads as a count rather than a second label.
  • Prototype rows have no chevron. The trailing status glyph, building shown in green, is their only trailing mark.
A Stacked sections, top-aligned (the chosen layout; reference)

One scroll, two labelled sections: Themes 8 then Prototypes 5, each with its own + . The header collapses its section, so its chevron is legitimate (shown at rest only while collapsed; on approach otherwise). Headers stick: a section below the fold docks its header at the bottom edge, and clicking a docked header scrolls to it instead of collapsing.

  • Both populations visible at once; prototypes sit directly under themes instead of being pushed to the floor.
  • With many themes, prototypes fall below the fold. Sticky headers keep the section findable (docked at the bottom), at the cost of one dual-purpose click.
  • Creation moves from the brand row onto each section, beside its object. The brand row loses its ambiguous +.
  • Collapsing is a session preference worth remembering, like the rail itself.
B Tabs: Themes | Prototypes

A segmented switch under the brand row; one list at a time slides in from its side. The brand row's + creates whatever the visible tab lists. The tab follows the pane: opening a theme from the view swaps back to Themes. A green dot on a hidden tab says it holds something new.

  • Most compact and calm; long lists get the whole height.
  • Hides a population. While a prototype is open, the theme index (and its scroll-spy and fresh marks) is out of sight; the tab dot is the only trace.
  • A tab that follows the pane can swap the list under the pointer; a tab that doesn't leaves the open object unlisted.
  • The theme index is a table of contents for the view; tabs make it optional chrome.
C Bands, after Columns (the chosen headers; reference)

Columns' grammar turned 90°: each type gets a band with Columns' header (type icon, name, count, +) and its own scroll, raw to refined from top to bottom. Short lists sit top-aligned; long ones share the height, so the Prototypes band never leaves the screen. No collapse, so no chevrons at all.

  • Both populations always on screen, at any size, without a sticky trick.
  • Two scroll regions in 212 px. Each band can be cut mid-list; the header's hairline shows when a band is scrolled.
  • Type icons in the headers match Columns and the mini rail, so headers carry identity and rows stay text.
  • Split is proportional to population; a draggable seam (as the pane edge) would be the next step if it feels wrong.
D Prototypes become a section of the view

A grammar fix rather than a layout one: every sidebar row is a table-of-contents entry for the view. Themes scroll to their sections; a Prototypes entry follows them and scrolls to a Prototypes section after the last theme, where prototype cards open in the pane like theory cards do.

  • One verb per sidebar row (go to section); the current mix of "scroll the view" and "open in the pane" disappears.
  • Prototypes gain a home with room for status and the theory they test, without a view of their own.
  • Individual prototypes are no longer one click from the sidebar; reaching one is a scroll or ⌘K.
  • Changes the base plane, so it needs a design-model update (the "Prototypes link at the bottom of the index" line).
